## Make the paint look shiny

Car paint is glossy, so it reflects light in long streaks:

1. **Leave white streaks** along the curved parts of the car: the roof, the bonnet (or hood) and the tops of the fenders. Long, thin streaks look best.
2. **Go darker at the bottom.** Colour the lower half of the car a little darker than the top. It makes the car look rounded and sitting on the road.
3. **Try two-tone.** Lots of classic cars were painted one colour on top and another below, or had stripes, like the striped beetle.

## Chrome, glass and tyres

- **Chrome bumpers and hubcaps:** colour them light grey with white streaks, and add a thin edge of dark blue-grey, because chrome reflects the sky above it and the road below.
- **Windows:** light blue with one diagonal white streak across the glass.
- **Tyres:** dark grey rather than pure black, so the tread still shows, with a lighter line around the side of the tyre.
- **Headlights:** pale yellow in the middle, with a white dot for the shine.

In the Colouring Studio, the *Robots & cars* colour set has chrome, tyre black, taxi yellow and racing red, and the alcohol markers are good for smooth, layered paint.

## Car games for kids

For road trips, waiting rooms and rainy afternoons:

- **Car colour bingo:** draw a grid of colours and tick them off as you spot a car of each one.
- **Design a number plate:** give your favourite car from the book its own licence plate.
- **Invent a car:** a name, a colour, and one thing no real car can do (it floats, it flies, it makes pancakes).
- **Classic car detective:** count round headlights, tail fins and chrome bumpers on the cars you pass.

If your child enjoys these, the [Cars Coloring Book](../../cars-coloring-book/) has 40 cars to colour, one per page, from beetles and minis to race cars, campers and trucks.
